Manali Weather & Snowfall Guide 2026

📅 Updated: August 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read · ✍️ By Kumar Raj

Manali sits at 2,050 meters (6,726 feet) in the Kullu Valley of Himachal Pradesh. Unlike Kedarnath which closes for winter, Manali is a year-round destination — each season offers something completely different. Snow in winter, adventure sports in summer, lush greenery in monsoon, and golden autumns. This guide covers everything: month-by-month weather, snowfall patterns, what to wear, and the best time for YOUR kind of trip.

📊 Manali Weather — Month by Month

❄️ January HEAVY SNOW
-5°C to 8°C
Peak winter. Heavy snowfall. Solang Valley turns into a ski paradise. Rohtang Pass closed. Perfect for honeymooners and snow lovers. Hotels at peak prices. Carry heavy woolens — temperature drops below freezing at night. Best for: Snow, skiing, honeymoon.
❄️ February SNOW
-3°C to 10°C
Snow continues. Less crowded than January. Great for skiing and snow activities. Roads to Solang are open (snow-cleared). Rohtang still closed. Budget travelers can find better hotel deals than January. Best for: Snow on a budget.
🌸 March TRANSITION
2°C to 15°C
Snow begins melting. Pleasant days, cold nights. Snow still visible on peaks but roads are clear. Good time for sightseeing without extreme cold. Fewer tourists. Hotel prices moderate. Best for: Peaceful sightseeing.
✅ April – May BEST
8°C to 25°C
Perfect weather! Pleasant days, cool nights. All roads open. Solang Valley offers paragliding, zorbing, cable car. Rohtang opens in late May. Peak tourist season starts mid-May. Book hotels 2-3 weeks ahead. Best for: Adventure sports, families, first-time visitors.
✅ June PEAK
12°C to 28°C
Warmest month. Escape the plains heat! Rohtang Pass fully open. All adventure sports operating. Maximum crowds — expect traffic jams at Solang and Rohtang. Hotel prices at their highest. Best for: Escaping summer heat, Rohtang Pass.
🌧️ July – August MONSOON
15°C to 25°C
Rainy season. Lush green valleys, waterfalls everywhere. Landslides possible on mountain roads — check conditions before driving. Fewer tourists, cheaper hotels. Good for budget travelers willing to risk rain. Best for: Budget travel, photography, greenery.
🍂 September – October BEST VALUE
8°C to 22°C
Post-monsoon magic. Clear skies, lush green after rains. September is the sweet spot — good weather, fewer crowds, cheaper hotels. October brings golden autumn colors. Nights start getting cold by late October. Best for: Budget + good weather combo.
❄️ November FIRST SNOW
0°C to 15°C
Winter begins. First snowfall in late November at higher elevations (Solang, Rohtang). Days are pleasant but nights are cold. Very few tourists. Great deals on hotels. Rohtang closes by mid-November. Best for: Peaceful getaway, early snow chasers.
🎄 December SNOW + CHRISTMAS
-3°C to 10°C
Winter wonderland! Snow-covered Manali is magical. Christmas and New Year week is PEAK — book months ahead. Solang has skiing. Old Manali cafes are cozy with bonfires. Best for: Christmas/New Year celebration, honeymoon, snow.

❄️ Manali Snowfall Guide

Manali itself (2,050m) gets snowfall in winter, but the real snow action is at Solang Valley (2,560m) and Rohtang Pass (3,978m):

  • December – February: Peak snow season. Solang Valley becomes a ski resort. Snow depth: 2-5 feet at Solang, 5-10 feet at Rohtang. Manali town gets 1-2 feet of snow.
  • March: Snow begins melting. Skiing still possible in early March at Solang. Slushy by late March.
  • April – June: No snow in Manali town. Lingering snow on Rohtang peaks — great for snow photos without the cold.
  • July – October: No snow anywhere. Green meadows at Solang.
  • November: First light snowfall at Solang usually by late November. Manali town gets its first dusting.

👕 What to Wear in Manali — Season by Season

Winter (Dec–Feb) — Heavy Snow

  • Thermal innerwear (top + bottom)
  • Heavy down jacket or parka
  • Waterproof snow boots with good grip
  • Woolen cap, gloves, muffler, earmuffs
  • Multiple layers: thermal → sweater → fleece → jacket
  • Sunglasses — snow blindness is real on bright days

Summer (Mar–Jun) — Pleasant Days

  • Light t-shirts and cotton clothes for daytime (15-28°C)
  • Light jacket or sweater for evenings (drops to 8-12°C)
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Sunglasses, sunscreen (SPF 50+), sun hat
  • Carry one warm layer for Rohtang Pass (still cold up there!)

Monsoon (Jul–Aug) — Rainy

  • Quick-dry clothing — avoid cotton
  • Raincoat or heavy poncho
  • Waterproof shoes or sandals with grip
  • Umbrella for town walks
  • Light sweater for evenings

Autumn (Sep–Nov) — Crisp & Cool

  • Layers: t-shirt → sweater → light jacket
  • Warm jacket for November evenings (near 0°C)
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Woolen cap for November mornings
