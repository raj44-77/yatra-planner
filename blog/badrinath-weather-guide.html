Badrinath Weather Guide 2026

📅 Updated: July 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read · ✍️ By Kumar Raj

Badrinath sits at 3,300 meters (10,827 feet) in the Garhwal Himalayas — slightly lower than Kedarnath. The biggest difference? No trekking required — the road goes all the way to the temple gate. But the weather is still harsh Himalayan weather. This guide covers everything: month-by-month temperatures, snowfall, monsoon, what to wear, and how Badrinath's weather compares to Kedarnath.

Key takeaway: Badrinath is slightly warmer and much more accessible than Kedarnath. If you're worried about the Kedarnath trek or traveling with elderly family, Badrinath is the safer weather choice.

🌡️ Temperature Guide

  • Summer days (May-June): 18-22°C — comfortable in a light jacket or sweater
  • Summer nights: 8-12°C — need a warm jacket
  • Monsoon (July-August): 12-20°C — humid, wet, rain gear essential
  • Autumn (September-October): 4-20°C — pleasant days, cold nights
  • Winter (November-April): -12°C to 14°C — temple closed, road blocked

❄️ Badrinath Snowfall Guide

  • April: Snow on surrounding peaks. Road cleared by BRO (Border Roads Organisation). No snow on the road.
  • May–June: No snow. Clear roads.
  • July–August: No snow. Rain instead.
  • September–October: First light snow possible in late October at higher elevations. Road still open.
  • November–March: Heavy snowfall. Road closed. Temple buried under snow.

🌧️ Monsoon Guide — Is Badrinath Safe During Rains?

Badrinath has a slight advantage over Kedarnath during monsoon because there's no trekking — you're in a vehicle on the road. But:

  • Landslides are the main risk: The Joshimath-Badrinath road (NH-7) is vulnerable to landslides during heavy rain. BRO works continuously to keep it open, but delays of 2-6 hours are common.
  • Road closures: In extreme rain, the road may close for 1-2 days. Always check with locals in Joshimath before proceeding.
  • Helicopters: Available from Dehradun to Badrinath but stop during bad weather.
  • Our recommendation: Avoid July–August if possible. September is the sweet spot — post-monsoon greenery with minimal rain risk.

👕 What to Wear in Badrinath — Month by Month

May–June (Summer Peak)

  • Light layers for daytime. T-shirt with a light jacket or sweater.
  • Warm jacket for evenings and early mornings.
  • Comfortable walking shoes — no trekking boots needed (road access!).
  • Sunglasses, sunscreen (SPF 50+), sun hat.

July–August (Monsoon)

  • Raincoat or heavy poncho — essential.
  • Quick-dry clothing. Avoid cotton.
  • Waterproof shoes or sandals with good grip.
  • Umbrella — useful for temple visits between rain spells.

September–October (Autumn)

  • Layers: Thermal → Sweater → Jacket. Remove layers during the day.
  • Heavy jacket for nights (below 5°C by October).
  • Woolen cap and gloves for early morning at Tapt Kund.
