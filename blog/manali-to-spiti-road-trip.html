Manali to Spiti Valley Road Trip Guide 2026

📅 Updated: August 2026 · ⏱️ 12 min read · ✍️ By Kumar Raj

🏔️ One of India's Greatest Road Trips: The Manali to Spiti Valley road trip is legendary — approximately 200 km of some of the most breathtaking and challenging terrain in the Himalayas. You cross two high passes (Rohtang at 3,978m and Kunzum at 4,590m), drive alongside the Chandra River, and enter a landscape that looks like another planet. This guide covers everything: the complete route, permits you'll need, budget estimates, bike vs car, places to visit, a 6-day itinerary, and critical safety tips.

🗺️ The Route: Manali → Spiti Valley (Kaza) — Stop by Stop

Total Distance: ~200 km | Driving Time: 8-10 hours (non-stop) | Road Type: Mix of paved and unpaved mountain roads

📍 0 km
Manali (2,050m) — Starting Point
Fill fuel before departing. There are petrol pumps in Manali town. Stock up on water, snacks, and basic medicines at Manali market. Get your Rohtang permit sorted before leaving (see permit section). Start by 5-6 AM to cross Rohtang before tourist traffic builds up. The road begins with a steep climb through pine forests.
📍 38 km
Rohtang Pass (3,978m) — Gateway to Lahaul
First high pass. Permit check post here — have your Rohtang permit ready (digital or printed copy, as mobile network is unreliable). Snow on the pass even in June and July. Stop for photos — the view of glaciers and surrounding peaks is unforgettable. Small dhabas serve hot chai and Maggi. Basic toilets available. The pass can get crowded with day tourists from Manali between 10 AM-3 PM — crossing early avoids this.
📍 60 km
Gramphu — Important Road Junction
Key decision point. The road splits here. The left fork goes to Leh (via Keylong and Jispa). The RIGHT fork goes to Spiti/Kaza. Take the right fork. The road surface becomes noticeably rougher after this junction — this is where the real Spiti adventure begins. A small police check post may note down your vehicle number.
📍 75 km
Chhatru — First Rest Stop
Small seasonal settlement with a few basic dhabas and tented accommodation. Good place for a tea break or early lunch. Paratha, dal-rice, and chai are typically available during the tourist season (June-September). The landscape changes dramatically after Chhatru — greenery fades and the mountains become bare, rugged, and beautifully stark.
📍 90 km
Batal — Last Stop Before Kunzum Pass
A tiny outpost with 2-3 basic dhabas and a PWD rest house. This is a popular overnight stop for travelers breaking the journey. The famous Chandra Dhaba here is known among bikers for hot meals and travel stories. Chandratal Lake detour starts from near Batal — a 14 km rough road branches off here. Fill water bottles here — the next reliable stop is after Kunzum Pass.
📍 110 km
Kunzum Pass (4,590m) — The Highest Point
The crown of the journey. At 4,590 meters, this is the highest motorable pass on this route. The Kunzum Mata Temple sits at the top — it's a tradition among travelers to stop here, offer prayers, and circumambulate the temple for a safe journey. Breathtaking 360° views of the Chandra-Bhaga mountain range. It can be very cold and windy here even in peak summer — keep a warm jacket handy. Short photo stop recommended (10-15 minutes) due to the altitude. The pass is closed from approximately October to May due to heavy snow.
📍 130 km
Chandratal Lake Detour (Optional — 14 km from main road)
One of India's most beautiful high-altitude lakes. Crescent-shaped, with deep blue-green water at 4,300m, surrounded by bare mountains. The 14 km access road from near Batal is rough and takes about 1-1.5 hours each way. A 4WD vehicle or sturdy bike is recommended. Basic tented accommodation is available near the lake during season (₹500-1,500/night). A forest camping permit may be required if staying overnight. The lake looks different at different times of day — if you can, visit in the early morning for mirror-like reflections.
📍 160 km
Losar — First Village of Spiti
The first proper village after crossing into Spiti Valley. Altitude: 4,080m. A few homestays and basic dhabas are available here. Traditional whitewashed Spitian houses with flat roofs — a distinct architectural change from the Kullu Valley. A good place to rest if the altitude is affecting you. Locals are warm and welcoming.
📍 200 km
🏁 Kaza (3,800m) — You Made It!
Spiti's main town and your base for exploration. Kaza has petrol pumps (check locally for operational status), ATMs (SBI is most reliable), hotels ranging from budget to comfortable, guesthouses, cafes, and a charming market area. From Kaza, you can explore Key Monastery, Kibber, Langza, Hikkim, and Komic over the next few days. Congratulations — you've completed one of India's most epic road trips! 🏔️

🗓️ Suggested 6-Day Manali to Spiti Itinerary

Day 1
Manali → Chhatru / Batal (75-90 km)
Start early (5-6 AM) from Manali. Cross Rohtang Pass before the tourist rush. Stop at Gramphu for tea. Continue to Chhatru or push to Batal depending on your pace and how you're feeling. Stay at a basic dhaba or tented camp (₹500-1,200). The change in landscape from green Kullu to stark Lahaul is dramatic and beautiful.
Day 2
Batal → Chandratal Lake → Kunzum Pass → Kaza (130 km)
Early morning: Take the detour to Chandratal Lake (if road conditions permit). Spend 1-2 hours at this stunning high-altitude lake. Return to the main road and cross Kunzum Pass. Stop at Kunzum Mata Temple. Continue through Losar village and reach Kaza by evening. Check into your hotel/guesthouse (₹800-2,500/night). Walk around Kaza market in the evening to acclimatize.
Day 3
Kaza → Key Monastery → Kibber → Kaza (40 km round trip)
Morning visit to the magnificent Key Monastery (12 km from Kaza). This 1,000-year-old monastery perched on a hilltop is the iconic image of Spiti. Spend 1-2 hours exploring. Continue to Kibber Village — one of the highest inhabited villages in the region. Keep an eye out for wildlife (ibex, blue sheep, maybe even a snow leopard in winter). Return to Kaza by late afternoon. Try the local cafes in the evening.
Day 4
Kaza → Langza → Hikkim → Komic → Kaza (50 km loop)
The fossil and village circuit. Langza: Giant Buddha statue with a stunning mountain backdrop. The area is rich in marine fossils — you may find small ones on the ground. Hikkim: Visit the small post office often called the world's highest. Send a postcard home — it's a unique souvenir. Komic: One of the highest villages connected by road, with the Tangyud Monastery. Return to Kaza by evening.
Day 5
Kaza → Losar → Kunzum Pass → Batal / Chhatru (120 km)
Begin the return journey. Stop at Losar for a final look at Spiti's first village. Cross Kunzum Pass again — the views in the opposite direction feel completely new. Stay overnight at Batal or Chhatru. If you missed Chandratal on Day 2, this is your second chance on the return leg.
Day 6
Batal / Chhatru → Rohtang Pass → Manali (100 km)
Final leg of the journey. Cross Rohtang Pass early to avoid tourist traffic from Manali. The sight of green forests returning as you descend towards Manali feels like coming home. Reach Manali by afternoon. Celebrate completing one of India's most unforgettable road trips! 🏔️

🏔️ Places to Explore Around Kaza (Spiti Valley)

  • Key Monastery (12 km from Kaza): Over 1,000 years old, this iconic monastery sits dramatically on a hilltop overlooking the Spiti River. Home to hundreds of monks. The morning prayer ceremony is a special experience.
  • Kibber Village (20 km): A high-altitude village with traditional stone houses. Good homestay options if you want to experience local life. The area is known for wildlife spotting.
  • Langza (15 km): Famous for the giant golden Buddha statue set against Himalayan peaks. The area is geologically rich — marine fossils can be found here, evidence that Spiti was once under the sea.
  • Hikkim (16 km): Home to a small post office at high altitude. Sending a postcard from here is a popular traveler ritual.
  • Komic (18 km): One of the highest villages with road access. The Tangyud Monastery here offers incredible panoramic views.
  • Chandratal Lake: A stunning crescent-shaped blue lake at 4,300m. Best visited on the way to or from Manali. Camping near the lake under a star-filled sky is an unforgettable experience.

⚠️ Important Tips for the Journey

  1. Give yourself time to acclimatize: Spend at least 1-2 days in Manali (2,050m) before heading over 4,500m passes. Altitude sickness symptoms include headache, nausea, and dizziness. If symptoms are severe, descend to a lower altitude.
  2. Start early each day: Mountain weather is typically clearer in the morning. Afternoon clouds, wind, or rain are more common. Aim to be off the high passes by early afternoon.
  3. Mobile network is very limited: Most of the route has no signal. BSNL sometimes works near Kaza. Inform family before leaving Manali that you may be unreachable for several days.
  4. Carry sufficient cash: ATMs in Kaza may not always be operational. Carry enough cash for your entire trip from Manali.
  5. Pack for all weather: You can experience sun, rain, wind, and near-freezing temperatures on the same day. Pack layers, a warm jacket, rain gear, gloves, and a cap.
  6. Carry basic medicines: Include altitude sickness medication (consult a doctor), pain relievers, band-aids, antiseptic cream, and ORS packets. Pharmacies are very limited on this route.
  7. Vehicle preparation: If renting, check brakes, tires (including spare), lights, and horn. Carry basic tools and a puncture repair kit. A vehicle breakdown in Spiti can be challenging.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Manali to Spiti Valley (Kaza)?

The distance is approximately 200 km. The drive takes 8-10 hours without long stops. Most travelers prefer to break the journey with an overnight halt at Chhatru or Batal, making it a comfortable 2-day drive each way.

Can I travel from Manali to Spiti by bike?

Yes, biking is one of the most popular ways to do this route. A Royal Enfield (Himalayan, Classic 350/500) handles the terrain well. Rentals are available in Manali (₹1,200-1,500/day). Riders should have prior mountain riding experience. Be prepared for cold wind, especially on the passes.

Is a permit required for the Manali to Spiti road trip?

Yes, a Rohtang Pass permit is typically required for all vehicles crossing the pass. Check the latest regulations on the official Himachal tourism portal or at the SDM office in Manali before your trip. Foreign nationals may need an Inner Line Permit for certain areas near the border.

What is the best month for the Manali to Spiti trip?

June and September are generally considered the best months. June offers fresh snow on the passes and green landscapes. September offers clear skies, golden autumn colors, and stable roads. July and August have some landslide risk on the Manali-Rohtang section. The route is closed from approximately November to May due to snow on the high passes.

Is the Manali to Spiti road trip safe for beginners?

With preparation, yes. The road is rough in sections but does not require technical off-roading skills. Key risks to prepare for: high altitude (acclimatize in Manali first), unpredictable weather (pack layers), limited mobile connectivity (inform contacts beforehand), and long stretches without services (carry water, snacks, and basic tools).

How many days are enough for Spiti Valley from Manali?

Minimum 5 days, ideal 6-7 days for a round trip from Manali. This allows 2 days travel each way and 2-3 days to explore Kaza and the surrounding villages like Key, Kibber, Langza, Hikkim, and Komic.

Can I visit Chandratal Lake on the Manali-Spiti route?

Yes. Chandratal Lake is accessed via a 14 km side road that branches off from near Batal. The detour takes about 1-1.5 hours each way. It's best visited either on the onward journey (Day 2) or return journey (Day 5). Tented accommodation is available near the lake during the tourist season.

How much does a Manali to Spiti trip cost?

For a 6-day trip: approximately ₹14,000-22,000 per person by bike (including rental, fuel, food, and basic accommodation). For a group of 4 sharing a car: approximately ₹12,500-20,000 per person.
