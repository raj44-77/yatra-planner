Manali to Leh Road Trip Guide 2026

📅 Updated: August 2026 · ⏱️ 14 min read · ✍️ By Kumar Raj

🏔️ The Ultimate Himalayan Road Trip: The Manali to Leh highway is one of the highest and most spectacular roads in the world. Spanning approximately 470 km, this route crosses four high-altitude mountain passes — Rohtang La (3,978m), Baralacha La (4,890m), Nakee La (4,739m), and Tanglang La (5,328m) — the latter being one of the highest motorable passes on the planet. The road is open only for a few months each year (typically June to September) and the journey takes you through landscapes that shift from green forests to barren moonscapes. This guide covers everything you need to know: the complete route, permits, budget, bike vs car, a 7-day itinerary, and essential safety tips.

🗺️ The Route: Manali → Leh — Stop by Stop (470 km)

Total Distance: ~470 km | Driving Time: 2 days (with overnight halt) | Road Type: Mix of paved, broken, and unpaved sections

📍 0 km
Manali (2,050m) — Starting Point
Fill fuel in Manali town where multiple petrol pumps are available. Stock up on water, snacks, basic medicines, and warm clothing. Get your Rohtang permit sorted before departure (see permit section). Start by 5-6 AM to cross Rohtang Pass before the tourist rush. The journey begins with a steep climb through pine and deodar forests.
📍 38 km
Rohtang Pass (3,978m) — First Major Pass
The first of four high passes on this route. Permit check post here — have documents ready. Snow can be present even in July. The pass offers panoramic views of glaciers and peaks. Basic dhabas serve hot tea and snacks. Toilets available. The pass can get crowded with day tourists between 10 AM-3 PM — crossing early is strongly recommended.
📍 60 km
Gramphu — Road Junction
Key decision point. The road splits: left fork continues to Leh via Keylong. Right fork goes to Spiti/Kaza. Take the LEFT fork for Leh. A police check post may register your vehicle details here. The landscape begins its dramatic transformation from green Kullu Valley to the stark, arid mountains of Lahaul.
📍 85 km
Keylong (3,080m) — District Headquarters of Lahaul
The largest town in the Lahaul region. Good place for a lunch break. Multiple dhabas and small restaurants serve hot meals. Basic hotels and guesthouses available if you decide to break the journey here. Petrol pump may be available — check locally. ATMs are limited. The Kardang Monastery, visible on a hill across the Bhaga River, is worth a short visit.
📍 110 km
Jispa (3,200m) — Popular Overnight Stop
A beautiful village on the banks of the Bhaga River. This is the most popular place to spend the first night. Several campsites and guesthouses offer riverside accommodation (₹800-2,500/night). Jispa is at a comfortable altitude for acclimatization compared to Sarchu. The sunset over the river with mountains in the background is stunning. Book in advance during peak season (July-August).
📍 145 km
Darcha (3,360m) — Last Village Before the High Passes
A small settlement with a police check post. This is the last point with any significant habitation before the long, desolate stretch to Sarchu. Basic dhabas available. Some travelers prefer to stay here instead of Jispa for an earlier start over Baralacha La the next morning.
📍 180 km
Baralacha La (4,890m) — Second High Pass
One of the most dramatic passes on the route. At nearly 4,900 meters, Baralacha La is a vast, windswept plateau where three mountain ranges — the Himalayas, Zanskar, and Pir Panjal — meet. The Suraj Tal (Sun Lake) near the pass is a stunning deep-blue lake worth a photo stop. The pass can be very cold and windy at any time. Short stop recommended due to the altitude. The descent from Baralacha La towards Sarchu is a breathtaking drive through a wide, barren valley.
📍 220 km
Sarchu (4,290m) — The Halfway Point
The traditional overnight stop between Manali and Leh. Sarchu is a vast, flat valley that marks the border between Himachal Pradesh and Ladakh. Numerous tented camps operate here during the season (₹800-2,000/night with meals). Important altitude note: Sarchu is at 4,290m — significantly higher than Jispa. Some travelers experience altitude sickness here. If you're already feeling unwell at Jispa, consider pushing through to Leh instead of stopping at Sarchu. The night sky at Sarchu, with zero light pollution, is one of the best stargazing experiences in India.
📍 255 km
Nakee La (4,739m) — Third Pass
A lesser-known but spectacular pass. The climb from Sarchu is through a series of 21 hairpin bends known as the Gata Loops — one of the most photographed sections of the Manali-Leh highway. The view from the top looking back at the loops is unforgettable. The pass itself is marked by prayer flags fluttering in the strong wind.
📍 300 km
Pang (4,600m) — Fuel and Food Stop
A small outpost with a few dhabas and tented camps. This is a common lunch stop. The dhabas serve basic but hot meals — dal-rice, Maggi, and chai. A petrol pump may be available here — check locally as availability can vary. The landscape around Pang is starkly beautiful — wide plains surrounded by colorful mountains.
📍 360 km
Tanglang La (5,328m) — The Highest Point
The crown of the journey. Tanglang La is the highest pass on the Manali-Leh highway and one of the highest motorable passes in the world. The approach is a long, gradual climb through a vast mountain desert. At the top, the sense of achievement is immense. The views stretch for miles in every direction — endless mountains under a deep blue sky. Oxygen levels are significantly lower here. Keep your stop brief — 10-15 minutes is enough for photos and soaking in the moment. The descent towards Leh is a smooth, scenic drive through the Indus Valley.
📍 420 km
Upshi — Entering the Indus Valley
The first significant settlement on the Ladakh side. An army check post verifies permits here. The landscape becomes greener as you enter the Indus Valley. The famous Upshi Bridge over the Indus River is a popular photo stop. From here, Leh is just 50 km away on good roads.
📍 470 km
🏁 Leh (3,500m) — You Made It!
Welcome to Ladakh! After approximately 470 km and crossing four of the world's highest passes, you've arrived in Leh — the Land of High Passes. Check into your hotel or guesthouse (₹800-5,000/night). Rest well — your body needs time to adjust to the altitude. Explore Leh Market in the evening if you're feeling up to it. You've just completed one of the most epic road trips on the planet! 🏔️

🗓️ Suggested 7-Day Manali to Leh Itinerary

Day 1
Arrive in Manali — Rest & Acclimatization
Arrive in Manali. Spend the day resting and acclimatizing at 2,050m. Get your permits sorted (Rohtang + ILP). Check your vehicle thoroughly. Stock up on supplies. Enjoy a relaxed evening on Mall Road or in Old Manali. Don't skip this day — proper acclimatization significantly reduces altitude sickness risk on the high passes.
Day 2
Manali → Jispa (110 km)
Start at 5-6 AM. Cross Rohtang Pass before tourist rush. Stop at Keylong for lunch. Reach Jispa by late afternoon. Check into a riverside camp or guesthouse. Walk along the Bhaga River in the evening. Sleep early — tomorrow is a long day.
Day 3
Jispa → Sarchu (110 km)
Start early (6 AM). Cross Baralacha La (4,890m). Stop at Suraj Tal for photos. Descend into the vast Sarchu plains. Reach by early afternoon. Rest, hydrate, and acclimatize to 4,290m. Watch for altitude symptoms. The night sky here is spectacular — step out after dark for unforgettable stargazing.
Day 4
Sarchu → Leh (250 km — Long Day)
Start very early (5 AM). Drive the famous Gata Loops (21 hairpin bends). Cross Nakee La (4,739m). Stop at Pang for lunch. Cross Tanglang La (5,328m) — the highest point of the journey. Descend through the Indus Valley. Reach Leh by late afternoon. Check into your hotel. Rest — you've earned it.
Day 5
Leh — Rest & Explore Local
Take it easy. Your body needs to adjust to Leh's 3,500m altitude. Walk around Leh Market. Visit Shanti Stupa for panoramic sunset views. Try Ladakhi food — momos, thukpa, butter tea. Leh Palace is worth a visit. This is a recovery and exploration day.
Day 6
Leh — Khardung La (Optional Day Trip)
If you're feeling well-acclimatized, do a day trip to Khardung La (5,359m) — one of the world's highest motorable passes. It's 40 km from Leh. The views of the Karakoram Range are breathtaking. Return to Leh by afternoon. Alternatively, visit Thiksey Monastery and Shey Palace in the Indus Valley.
Day 7
Leh — Departure or Return Journey
If flying back: Take a flight from Leh Airport to Delhi. If riding/driving back: Start the return journey on the same Manali-Leh highway (reverse route). Many bikers prefer to ride back, making it a 10-12 day round trip from Manali. Celebrate completing one of the world's greatest road trips! 🏔️

⚠️ Essential Safety Tips for Manali to Leh Highway

  1. Acclimatization is critical: The route crosses passes over 5,000m. Spend at least 1-2 days in Manali (2,050m) before starting. Break the journey with overnight stops at Jispa (3,200m) and Sarchu (4,290m). Altitude sickness symptoms include headache, nausea, dizziness, and shortness of breath. If severe, descend immediately.
  2. Start early every day: Mountain weather is typically better in the morning. Afternoons can bring wind, clouds, or rain. Aim to cross high passes by early afternoon.
  3. Mobile network is very limited: Most of the route has no signal. BSNL and Airtel work patchily near Keylong and Leh. Inform family before leaving that you may be unreachable for 2-3 days.
  4. Fuel availability: Petrol pumps are available in Manali, Keylong (check locally), and Leh. There may be a pump at Pang — check locally. Fill up whenever you can. Many travelers carry extra fuel in jerry cans.
  5. Carry sufficient cash: ATMs are limited to Keylong and Leh. Carry enough cash for fuel, food, and accommodation for the entire journey.
  6. Pack for extreme weather: You can experience sun, rain, wind, and near-freezing cold on the same day. Pack thermal layers, a warm jacket, rain gear, gloves, cap, and sunglasses.
  7. Carry basic medicines: Include altitude sickness medication (consult a doctor), pain relievers, band-aids, antiseptic, ORS, and any personal prescriptions.
  8. Vehicle preparation: Check brakes, tires (including spare), lights, and horn before starting. Carry basic tools, a puncture repair kit, and extra engine oil if riding a bike.
  9. Respect the mountains: This is not a racetrack. Drive/ride at a safe speed. The road has claimed lives. Enjoy the journey, not just the destination.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Manali to Leh?

The Manali to Leh distance is approximately 470 km. The journey is typically done over 2 days with an overnight halt, making it a comfortable and safe trip.

Is the Manali to Leh highway open year-round?

No. The highway is typically open only from June to September/early October. The high passes (Rohtang, Baralacha La, Tanglang La) are buried under snow during winter. The Srinagar-Leh highway is an alternative route that sometimes opens earlier and closes later.

Do I need permits for Manali to Leh?

Yes. A Rohtang Pass permit is required for crossing Rohtang. An Inner Line Permit (ILP) is required for entering Ladakh. These can be obtained online or at designated offices. Check the latest regulations before your trip as requirements can change.

Can I do Manali to Leh by bike?

Yes, this is one of the most popular biking routes in the world. A Royal Enfield (Himalayan, Classic 350/500) is the most common choice. Rentals are available in Manali. Prior mountain riding experience is strongly recommended.

How many days are needed for Manali to Leh?

Minimum 2 days for the drive (with an overnight halt). A comfortable one-way trip with rest and exploration takes 5-7 days. A round trip (riding/driving back) takes 10-14 days.

Is the Manali-Leh road safe?

The road is challenging but generally safe for prepared travelers. Key risks include high altitude, unpredictable weather, and rough road conditions in some sections. With proper acclimatization, a well-maintained vehicle, and sensible driving, thousands of travelers complete this journey safely every year.

What is the highest pass on the Manali-Leh highway?

Tanglang La at 5,328 meters (17,480 feet) is the highest point on the Manali-Leh highway. It is one of the highest motorable passes in the world.

How much does a Manali to Leh road trip cost?

For a one-way trip: approximately ₹10,500-16,700 per person by bike, and ₹9,700-16,700 per person by car (group of 4). A round trip costs roughly double. See the budget table above for a detailed breakdown.
