Kedarnath Weather Guide 2026

📅 Updated: July 2026 · ⏱️ 10 min read · ✍️ By Kumar Raj

Kedarnath sits at 3,583 meters (11,755 feet) in the Garhwal Himalayas. Weather here is completely different from the plains. It can snow in May, rain in September, and temperatures drop to 5°C even in peak summer. This guide covers everything — month-by-month temperatures, snowfall patterns, monsoon risks, what to wear each month, and how to plan your trek around the weather.

🌡️ Temperature Guide — What to Expect

Kedarnath's temperature swings dramatically between day and night, even in summer:

  • Summer days (May-June): 15-20°C — pleasant for trekking in a light jacket
  • Summer nights: 5-10°C — need thermals, heavy jacket, and blankets
  • Monsoon (July-August): 10-18°C — humid, wet, rain gear essential
  • Autumn (September-October): 2-18°C — beautiful days, freezing nights
  • Winter (November-April): -15°C to 5°C — temple closed, area inaccessible

❄️ Kedarnath Snowfall Guide

Snow is a reality at Kedarnath even during the yatra season:

  • April–Early May: Lingering winter snow on the trek path. Some sections may be icy. Ponies still operate.
  • Late May–June: Snow melts from the trek path but remains on surrounding peaks. No snow on the trail.
  • July–August: No snow. Rain instead.
  • September–October: First snowfall usually occurs in late October. Early snow possible from mid-October.
  • November–March: Heavy snowfall. Temple buried under 3-6 feet of snow.

Vasuki Tal (4,150m): This glacial lake remains partially frozen until late June. If you're trekking to Vasuki Tal, expect snow on the path even in summer.

🌧️ Kedarnath Monsoon Guide — Is It Safe?

The monsoon hits Kedarnath from late June through August. Here's what you need to know:

  • Landslide risk is real: The Rishikesh-Sonprayag road is notorious for landslides during heavy rain. You may get stranded for hours or even a day.
  • Trek path becomes slippery: The 16 km stone path gets wet and mossy. Good trekking shoes with grip are non-negotiable.
  • Cloudbursts: Rare but devastating. The 2013 Kedarnath disaster was caused by a cloudburst. Always check weather alerts before starting the trek.
  • Helicopters stop: Bad weather grounds helicopters. Don't rely on them during monsoon.
  • Our recommendation: Avoid July and August if possible. If you must travel, check the live weather page daily, start trekking early (by 5 AM), and carry full rain gear.

👕 What to Wear in Kedarnath — Month by Month

May–June (Summer Peak)

  • Day trek: Light t-shirt or moisture-wicking layer. Sun hat, sunglasses, sunscreen (SPF 50+).
  • Evening/night: Thermal innerwear, fleece jacket, heavy down jacket. Temperature drops to 5°C.
  • Rain gear: Light poncho for unexpected showers.
  • Footwear: Waterproof trekking shoes with good grip. Break them in before the trip.

July–August (Monsoon)

  • Full rain suit or heavy-duty poncho — not disposable plastic raincoats.
  • Quick-dry clothing — nothing cotton (stays wet and causes chafing).
  • Waterproof backpack cover or dry bags for electronics.
  • Extra pair of socks in a sealed plastic bag.
  • Trekking shoes with aggressive grip — paths are slippery.

September–October (Autumn)

  • Layers are key: Thermal → T-shirt → Fleece → Light jacket. Remove layers as you warm up trekking.
  • Heavy jacket for nights (below 5°C by October).
  • Woolen cap and gloves — especially for early morning aarti.
  • Light rain gear just in case.

April (Opening Month)

  • Full winter gear: Thermal, fleece, heavy down jacket, waterproof outer layer.
  • Waterproof trekking boots — snow and slush on the path.
  • Trekking stick with a metal tip for icy sections.
  • Sunglasses — snow blindness is real on bright days.
